The rehearsal

Before the raffle took a single real entry, we ran a complete round on Ethereum mainnet with our own money — real USDC, real Chainlink randomness, at one fifty-thousandth of the size. It worked. Every transaction below is on the public chain and you can open all of them.

Settled and fully paid. 2,000 tickets sold, 100 winners drawn by Chainlink VRF, all 99 cash prizes delivered, and nothing left stranded in the contract.

The randomness

Winners are drawn by Chainlink VRF v2.5, which produces a random number together with a cryptographic proof that it was not tampered with. The proof is verified on-chain before our contract is allowed to use it. Nobody — not us, not a miner, not Chainlink — can pick the winners.

We deliberately do not use block data for this. A block proposer can see values like prevrandao before publishing, and could quietly re-roll a draw worth a million dollars. Chainlink costs more and is worth it.

What this does and does not prove

It proves the machine runs

Real USDC moved, real randomness arrived, the arithmetic came out exact, and the contract emptied itself to the last cent without anyone intervening.

It does not replace an audit

A working rehearsal shows the contract does the right thing when nobody is attacking it. That is a different question from whether it resists someone with a million dollars to win, and only a professional audit answers the second.
